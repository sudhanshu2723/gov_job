Coverage statement

The scope of this research, stated in full rather than implied by what happens to be present. Every claim in the atlas should be read against this.

This set out to cover every sector. It reached about five of sixteen with real verification. The rest deserves to be named rather than quietly padded with plausible-sounding rows.

Not researched at all: Railways in full (RRB NTPC, JE, ALP, Technician, Section Controller, RPF SI, DMRC/JMRC) · insurance beyond a single NIACL page (LIC AAO, NICL, UIIC, OICL, GIC) · postal and telecom (India Post, DoT JTO, BSNL) · teaching (CTET, REET, KVS, NVS, DSSSB) · courts and eCourts technical posts · municipal and development-authority roles including JDA Jaipur · central-ministry direct recruitments · every state PSC except Rajasthan · the whole overlooked-bodies list — EPFO, ESIC, BIS, UIDAI, AAI, NTRO, CERT-In, FSSAI, TRAI, Election Commission, NHAI.

Also unverified: most of the PSU list (only the GATE question was touched, thinly) · NIC, NIELIT, C-DAC, C-DOT, CSIR, IGCAR, NPCIL · SSC exams other than CGL · Navy and Air Force entries beyond AFCAT's age rules · Intelligence Bureau, NIA, CBI direct recruitment.

On the comparative sections you asked for: salary figures here are thin and mostly single-sourced. Nothing was verified on in-hand pay, allowances, pension, promotion timelines or transfer patterns. Applicant-to-vacancy ratios and cutoffs weren't gathered beyond SSC CGL's UR figures. So the difficulty ordering, work-life-balance and job-security rankings, and tier placements are my judgment applied to the verified facts — reasoned, but not research findings, and I haven't dressed them up as such.

The session's web-search budget is exhausted (107 agents used all 200 calls), so I couldn't close those gaps today. Railways and insurance alone would add more vacancies than everything in Tier 1 combined — worth a second scoped run.

How the remaining gap is represented here

Rather than leave those sectors out, each is catalogued above as a Not researched row on a hatched background — filter to them with the Not researched chip. Those rows carry what I know as background at low confidence, plus the exact page to verify against. Nothing in them is a 2026 finding. Use the Verified chip to see only what was actually checked.

Candidate volumes are the weakest column in the atlas. Only UPSC CSE 2026 has a figure verified this session (≈8.2 lakh applied, ≈5.49 lakh appeared, 13,343 cleared Prelims). Every other applicant number is an order-of-magnitude estimate from typical recent cycles, tagged estimate. Do not quote them.
Rank-to-post tables are indicative, not rules. Allocation shifts every year with vacancy mix, category, cadre preference and reserve lists. The ordering is stable; the exact numbers are not. Treat them as "roughly where this post sits", never as a target score.
If a follow-up run happens, the order should be: the MeitY cluster (NIC, NIELIT, C-DAC, CERT-In) because it is the densest concentration of genuinely CSE-native government work · then RRB JE (IT), the one Junior Engineer family that admits Computer Science · then the PSU discipline tables · then Railways NTPC and the insurance block for raw vacancy volume · then court technical posts and the Rajasthan High Court, which are local and almost uncontested.
